# Platform Formats for a First Test

Loaded by the skill's Phase 5 (starter creative). One section per platform the skill can recommend for a first founder-run test. Field limits are the platforms' own published specs; re-verify against the platform's current documentation when a limit is load-bearing (they change without notice). Structures are deliberately minimal - a first test wants the fewest moving parts that can still produce a decision, not a grown account's architecture.

## Google Search (responsive search ads)

**First-test structure:** one campaign, 2-3 ad groups, each ad group one tight keyword theme (the problem phrase, the category phrase, a rival-alternative phrase if that intent exists). One responsive search ad per ad group.

**Match types:** phrase and exact only for a small budget - broad match hands the budget to the algorithm before there is data to steer it. Add 10-20 negative keywords up front (free-intent words, job-seeker words, the obvious wrong industries) and prune search terms weekly.

**Field limits:**

| Field | Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Headlines | up to 15, 30 characters each | minimum 3; write 10+ so the platform has real combinations to assemble |
| Descriptions | up to 4, 90 characters each | minimum 2 |
| Display path | 2 fields, 15 characters each | reinforce the keyword, not the brand |

Headline coverage to aim for across the set: the pain phrased as the searcher types it, the outcome with a concrete number, the differentiator, a proof point, the offer/CTA, the category term itself (searchers scan for the words they just typed).

**Small-budget notes:** conversion volume will be too low for automated bidding to learn - start on manual or max-clicks bidding and judge from the search-terms report and conversion counts, not the platform's optimization score. Turn off search-partner and display expansion for the test; they blur the read.

## Meta (Facebook / Instagram)

**First-test structure:** one campaign, one or two ad sets (one audience each), 2-3 ads per ad set. More ad sets on a small budget splits the signal below anything readable.

**Field limits and truncation:**

| Field | Recommended | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Primary text | ~125 characters before the fold | mobile clips the rest behind "See more" - most users never expand it, so the first sentence carries the whole ad |
| Headline | 40 characters (27 for Feed) | the bolded line under the creative |
| Description | ~25-30 characters | not always shown; never put load-bearing copy here |

**Creative:** one square (1080x1080) and one vertical (1080x1920) version of each concept covers the placements that matter. A plain product screenshot with a claim overlaid outperforms polished brand creative often enough that it belongs in every first test.

**Small-budget notes:** the ad set needs roughly 50 optimization events in a rolling week to exit the learning phase - most first-test budgets never will, and that is fine: judge the test manually on cost per result and click-through, and do not edit the ad set mid-test (significant edits reset learning and blur the numbers).

## LinkedIn (sponsored content, single image)

**First-test structure:** one campaign, one audience built from the profile's ICP (job titles or function + seniority, industry, company size). No lookalikes, no expansion - the point of paying LinkedIn prices is exact targeting.

**Field limits and truncation:**

| Field | Recommended | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Introductory text | under 150 characters | truncates around 150 on desktop and earlier on mobile; 255 hard max |
| Headline | under 70 characters | truncates past 70 |

**Small-budget notes:** LinkedIn clicks are the most expensive of the four platforms here - the format only makes sense when the price and margin clear a high CPA (run the calculator's max-CAC number first). Its lead-gen forms convert cheaper than site visits but fill the pipeline with lower-intent leads; for a first test, send clicks to the real page so the read includes the page.

## X (promoted posts)

**Format:** a post is the ad - 280 characters, and short, plain, conversational copy outperforms ad-voice. Write it like the founder's own post, because it is one.

**Small-budget notes:** cheapest reach of the four for developer and technical audiences, with the noisiest conversion data (weak attribution). Use it to test message resonance (click-through) rather than CPA.

## Rival evidence: the public ad libraries

Before picking, check where rivals sustain spend - a rival paying for the same audience for months is evidence (not proof) the channel can work in this category:

- Meta Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library) - every active Meta ad, searchable by advertiser.
- Google Ads Transparency Center (adstransparency.google.com) - active Google ads by advertiser.
- LinkedIn ad library (linkedin.com/ad-library) - active LinkedIn ads by company.

Read their angles and offers as market context for the creative work - never copy their claims (unverifiable) or their compliance posture (unknown).
